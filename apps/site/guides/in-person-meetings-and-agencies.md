# Why I like in-person meetings, and how to get the best out of agencies

Outline for a new guide. First draft, written from the title for Hugh's comments.

- **Who it is for:** founders and marketing leads at consumer brands who work with one or more agencies
  (paid social, creative, PR, email, design) and feel they are not getting their best work.
- **The promise:** a way of working with your agency that gets better work for the same fee: a clear brief,
  shared numbers, a steady rhythm, and in-person time where it counts.
- **The argument:** most agency problems are relationship problems, not capability problems. Agencies do
  their best work for the clients they understand and trust. That trust is built faster in a room than on
  a call.

**4. Why in person**
**Some things do not travel well over video.**
- **Trust.** People give more of themselves to people they have met. That includes their honest opinion.
- **Nuance.** You notice hesitation, enthusiasm and confusion that a call hides.
- **Speed.** A decision that takes three calls and an email chain can take twenty minutes at a whiteboard.
- **Ideas.** The best ones often come after the agenda is finished, over coffee or on the walk out.
- **Context.** Seeing the warehouse, the shop or the team explains things no brief can.

**5. Be honest about the cost**
**In person is not always better.**
- It takes travel time and money, on both sides.
- Routine updates are faster on a call or in writing.
- Remote teams can work very well together once trust is there.
The point is not to meet in person all the time. It is to meet in person at the moments that set the
direction.

**6. When to meet in person**
| Meet in person | A call or email is fine |
|---|---|
| The kick-off with a new agency. | Weekly updates. |
| Planning the next quarter. | Reviewing a single asset. |
| A big launch or a change in strategy. | Booking and scheduling. |
| When results drop, or the relationship feels off. | Sharing reports. |
| Creative sessions: briefs, ideas, workshops. | Small approvals. |

**7. Making the time count**
**A good in-person session has a purpose and a written outcome.**
- Send the agenda and any data a few days before.
- Plan for half the time on the agenda and half on open discussion.
- Bring the people who make decisions, and the people who do the work.
- Show them something real: the product, the customer reviews, the shop floor.
- Write up decisions and owners the same day.

**8. Why agency relationships underperform**
**Most agencies are capable. Most briefs and set-ups are not.**
| What the client says | What is often true |
|---|---|
| "They do not understand our brand." | Nobody has spent time showing them. |
| "The work is generic." | The brief was generic. |
| "They are not proactive." | They do not know the goal or the numbers. |
| "Results are slow." | They do not have access to the data they need. |
| "We are just a small account to them." | They have not been given a reason to care. |

**9. Treat them like part of the team**
**Agencies do their best work for clients they know.**
- Introduce them to the wider team, not only marketing.
- Invite them to the moments that matter: launches, results, the quarterly plan.
- Share the bad news as well as the good.
- Credit them in front of your own team when the work lands.
This is not about being soft. It is about giving them what they need to do the job.

**10. The brief**
**A good brief is the cheapest way to get better work.**
- The goal, and the one number it should move.
- Who the customer is, in their own words: reviews, emails, survey answers.
- What has worked and what has not, with the data.
- What must be true: claims, legal, brand rules.
- Budget, timing and who signs off.
- What success looks like at the first review.
One or two pages. If it needs more, the goal is not clear yet.

**11. Share the numbers**
**An agency cannot help you grow a number it cannot see.**
- Give them the business numbers, not only the channel numbers: margin, repeat rate, stock.
- Agree up front which number you will judge them on, and where it comes from.
- Give them direct access to the tools they need, with the right permissions. [Check each platform's access settings rather than sharing logins.]
- Agree how data is handled, and keep customer data inside the business unless there is a reason to share it.

**12. One owner**
**Give the agency one person to work with.**
- One owner on your side who briefs, approves and answers questions.
- Agree how fast you will give feedback and approvals, and stick to it.
- If the founder needs to see things, agree when, so the work does not wait.
Slow approvals cost you more than a slow agency.

**13. A steady rhythm**
| How often | What | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Weekly | Short update: the number, what is running, what is blocked. | Call |
| Fortnightly | Creative or campaign review. | Call or in person |
| Monthly | Results and learnings, and next month's plan. | Call |
| Quarterly | Strategy, goals and the relationship itself. | In person |

**14. Giving feedback**
**Be specific, be quick, and say what is working.**
- Feed back against the brief and the goal, not personal taste.
- Say what you like before what you would change.
- Give one consolidated set of comments, not several people's notes.
- Say "this does not do X" rather than "make it pop".
- Ask them what they would do differently, and listen.

**15. Ask for their feedback too**
**The best agencies will tell you what you are doing wrong.**
- Ask each quarter: what could we do better as a client?
- Ask what they see working for other brands, without asking for anything confidential.
- Ask what they would test if it were their money.
If they never push back, you are not getting their full value.

**16. Pay fairly and clearly**
- Agree scope in writing: what is included, and what is extra.
- Pay on time. It changes how they prioritise you.
- If you add work, agree the fee before they start.
- Tie part of the fee to results only if you both control what drives them. [Take advice before changing a contract.]

**17. How to tell if it is working**
**Judge the work, the results and the relationship.**
- **The work:** is it getting better, and closer to the brief first time?
- **The results:** is the agreed number moving, after a fair run?
- **The relationship:** do they bring ideas without being asked? Do they tell you bad news early?
Write down your view each quarter, and share it with them.

**18. When to part ways**
**Leave cleanly, and only after you have fixed your side.**
- Check the brief, the data and the access first. Many problems are on the client side.
- Say what is not working, plainly, and give them a set time to fix it.
- If it still does not work, end it politely and in writing.
- Make sure you own your accounts, ad accounts, files and data before you start. [Check your contract.]

**20. The checklist**
- We met our agency in person at the start, and we meet each quarter.
- They have a written brief with one goal and one number.
- They can see the numbers they need, through proper access.
- There is one owner on our side, and approvals are quick.
- We have a weekly, monthly and quarterly rhythm.
- We give one set of specific feedback, and we ask for theirs.
- We review the work, the results and the relationship each quarter.

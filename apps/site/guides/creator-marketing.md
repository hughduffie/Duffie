# Creator marketing for consumer brands

Reworked script for the guide. It replaces the deck "Whitelisting and creator ads".

- **Who it is for:** founders and marketers at consumer brands who want creators to be a working part of
  their growth, not a one-off.
- **The promise:** find the right creators, brief them without scripting them, pay them fairly, and turn the
  best content into ads.
- **What changed from the old deck:** the subject is now creator marketing as a whole. Whitelisting
  (partnership ads) is one tactic, covered in slides 12 to 15, after finding, briefing and paying creators.
- Text in [square brackets] is for you to check or fill in before it goes out.

---

**1. Cover**
Free guide for founders and marketers
**Creator marketing for consumer brands**
How to find creators, brief them, pay them and turn their best content into ads.

**2. Before you start**
**What this guide covers**
- Where creators fit in a growth plan, and when it is too early.
- The ways to work with creators, from gifting to paid ads.
- Finding people your customer already trusts.
- The brief, the agreement and the money.
- Running creator content as ads: partnership ads, also called whitelisting.
- How to test whether it is working.
Reading time: about 15 minutes.

**3. The idea**
**People trust people more than they trust brands.**
Creator marketing is paying, or otherwise working with, people your customer already listens to, so your
product is shown in their voice and not yours.

**4. Where it fits**
**Creators do three jobs for a consumer brand**
- **Reach:** their audience sees your product from someone they chose to follow.
- **Content:** you get video and photography that looks like real life, which most brands struggle to make.
- **Ads:** the best of that content can run as paid ads, from their handle or yours.
One good creator relationship can do all three.

**5. When it is too early**
- You cannot yet say your message in one sentence. Fix that first, or creators will each say something different.
- You have no budget to put behind the content that works.
- You cannot fulfil the orders if it does work.
An honest caveat: results vary a lot by creator and by product. Start small and test.

**6. The ways to work with creators**
| | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| **Gifting** | Send product, no obligation. | Finding who likes you. Cheap, slow, unpredictable. |
| **Paid content** | Pay for a post, a reel or a set of assets. | Content you can plan around and reuse. |
| **Ambassadors** | A few creators on a longer agreement. | Consistency. The audience sees you more than once. |
| **Affiliates** | A commission on sales through their link or code. | Low upfront cost. Works best alongside a fee. |
| **Partnership ads** | Running their content as ads from their handle. | Scaling what already works. See slide 12. |

**7. Finding creators**
- Start with customers who already post about you.
- Look for people your customer already trusts, not the biggest following.
- Watch how they talk. Their tone becomes your ad's tone.
- Ask for three recent examples of work with other brands.
- Keep a simple sheet: name, handle, why them, status, what you agreed.

**8. The first message**
**Be clear about money and rights from the start.**
- Say what you would pay for and what rights you need.
- Say what you will and will not ask them to say.
- Keep it under 100 words.
> Write a short, friendly message to [creator], who posts about [topic]. I run [brand]. I would like to pay
> them for a post, and to run it as a partnership ad. Under 100 words. Say what I would pay for and what
> rights I need.

**9. The brief**
**What to hold firm on, and what to leave**
| Hold firm on | Leave to the creator |
|---|---|
| The claims you can and cannot make | The hook and the first line |
| Product accuracy and safety | Their voice and humour |
| Clear labelling as an ad. Rules differ by country. | The setting and the format |
| Usage rights and timeline | How the product looks in real life |

One page is enough. If you script it, you lose the reason you hired them.

**10. Rights, usage and pay**
**Get it in writing.**
- **Usage:** where you may use the content, and for how long.
- **Ad fee:** running their content as ads is often a separate fee from making it. [Agree a figure]
- **Exclusivity:** whether they can work with competitors, and for how long.
- **Edits:** whether you may cut or caption the content.
- **Payment:** the amount, the dates and what triggers it.
A one-page agreement is enough to start. [Take advice for larger deals.]

**11. Make the content work harder**
- Ask for the raw files as well as the finished post.
- Agree up front that you can cut it into shorter versions.
- Use it in email, on product pages and in organic social, not only in ads.
- Tell the creator how it performed. They will make better content next time.

**12. Turning creator content into ads**
**Run the ad as the creator.**
A partnership ad runs from the creator's handle, with their permission, using your budget and targeting. It
looks like their content, not an ad from you. "Whitelisting" is the older, informal name for the same thing.
It is one tactic within creator marketing, and it only works once the content is good.

**13. Two kinds of permission**
- **Post-level:** the creator gives you a code for one post, reel or story. The easiest place to start.
- **Account-level:** the creator lets you run ads from their account, with new or existing content, without a code each time.
The creator grants permission. They never share their login. If anyone asks you to share logins, do not.

**14. Set-up, step by step**
What you need: an ad account in Meta Ads Manager, a creator with a professional Instagram account, and a signed agreement.
1. The creator posts the content, or picks an existing post.
2. They turn on partnership ads for that post and send you the code. [Confirm the current Instagram menu names.]
3. In Ads Manager, create an ad, choose the partnership ad option and paste the code. [Confirm the exact steps.]
4. Check the preview shows the creator's handle and the paid partnership label.
5. Set your own budget, targeting and schedule.

**15. Watch out: codes expire**
- An ad that spent yesterday can stop today because a permission lapsed.
- Ask how long the code lasts, and note the date.
- Put the renewal in your calendar a week early.
- Keep a sheet: creator, post, code, expiry, ad name.

**16. Test it properly**
1. Run the creator's version against the same content from your own handle.
2. Same budget, same audience, same dates. Change the handle and nothing else.
3. Judge by cost per purchase, not likes.
4. Keep the creators that work, and ask them for more.

**17. A simple rhythm**
- Each month: approach five new creators, brief two or three.
- Each fortnight: put the best new content into your ad account as a test.
- Each quarter: move the creators who work onto a longer agreement, and stop the ones who do not.

**18. Before you start**
**Relationship first. Permission second. Money third.**

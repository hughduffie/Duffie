# Proper brand guidelines in Figma, in 30 minutes

Reworked script for the guide. It replaces two decks: "Use Claude Code to bring your brand book to life" and
"Why Claude Code is worth half an hour of your time".

- **Who it is for:** founders and marketers at consumer brands whose brand book lives in a PDF.
- **The promise:** a working set of brand guidelines in Figma, built with code, that the whole team can use.
  Consistency is the point, because consistency is what lets a small team move faster.
- **What changed from the old decks:** the outcome leads (brand guidelines that drive consistency and growth).
  Claude Code is the method, introduced on slide 7, not the headline. The "why it is worth your time" case is
  cut down to one slide.
- Text in [square brackets] is for you to check or fill in before it goes out.

---

**1. Cover**
Free guide for founders and marketers
**Proper brand guidelines in Figma, in 30 minutes**
Built with code, so every email, ad and deck comes out on brand.

**2. Where this starts**
**Great design drives a business. Most of it never leaves the deck.**
You paid for a brand. The people making the emails and ads on a Tuesday afternoon cannot use it.

**3. Why I am writing this**
**I have seen this from three sides**
- From my own startup: how much good design can do for a business.
- From inside brands: valuable brand work left in the deck, because the day-to-day team are not designers.
- From agency and consulting work: the methods designers and developers use to stay consistent at speed.
The change: those methods used to need a designer or a developer. Now you can set them up yourself.

**4. The problem**
**The brand book, and a normal Tuesday**
| What the brand book says | What happens on a Tuesday |
|---|---|
| A beautiful PDF or deck. | A marketer needs an email by 3pm. |
| Rules for logo, colour, type and tone. | They open a blank template and guess. |
| Written for people who know design. | It comes out a bit off, or the idea never gets made. |

**5. What it costs you**
**Inconsistency is a growth problem, not a design problem.**
- When the team is not sure something will look right, they do not try the idea. Fewer ideas tested means slower growth.
- Every asset goes back to a designer or the founder for fixes. That is your slowest queue.
- Customers see a slightly different brand in every channel, so it takes longer to be remembered.

**6. How designers solve it**
**Three things that scale consistency**
- **A library.** One source of truth for colours, fonts, spacing and logos, so nobody picks a hex code by eye.
- **Components.** Reusable building blocks: a button, an email header, an ad layout. Change one and every copy updates.
- **Scripts.** Small automations that resize, export and fill templates, so repeat work takes seconds.
This is what "guidelines built with code" means. The rules are stored as values a tool can read, not as pictures in a PDF.

**7. What is new**
**You can now build all three yourself.**
Claude Code connects to Figma. Give it your brand book and it sets up the library, the components and the
templates. You do not need to code. You describe what you want and review what it makes. [Check what your Figma plan allows]

**8. What you will have in 30 minutes**
**A guidelines file your team can use**
- Colour and type set up as Figma variables and styles.
- A guidelines page in six sections: logo, colour, type, voice, layout, examples.
- Three starter components: an email header, an ad layout and a slide.
- One real asset made from it, so you know it works.
[Test the timing on your own brand before you publish this claim.]

**9. Before you start (5 minutes)**
**Put your brand in one folder**
- Logo files, SVG or PNG.
- A note with your colours (names and hex codes) and your fonts.
- Three pieces of work you like, and three emails or posts that sound like you.
- The brand book itself, even if it is messy.
You also need a Claude plan that includes Claude Code and a Figma account. [Confirm current plans]

**10. Connect Claude to Figma (5 minutes)**
**One connection, done once**
1. Open Claude Code in your brand folder. You can start in the Claude app, with no terminal.
2. Add the Figma connector and sign in when Figma asks. [Exact steps from Figma's help page]
3. Check it works. Ask: "List the frames in this file: [link]". If it names them, you are connected.

**11. Build the system (15 minutes)**
**Prompt 1: build the guidelines**
> Here is our brand book and brand folder. In this Figma file [link], build our brand guidelines: colour and
> type variables, text styles, and a guidelines page with six sections (logo, colour, type, voice, layout,
> examples). Then build reusable components for an email header, an ad and a slide. Ask me anything that is
> unclear before you start.

Tips: start with one small system, not everything. Ask it what is missing from the brand book. Name things
clearly, because Claude and your team will both use the names.

**12. Your guidelines page, in six sections**
| Logo | Colour | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Versions, clear space, what not to do. | Names, hex codes and where each is used. | Heading and body fonts, with sizes. |

| Voice | Layout | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Five words that describe you, with examples. | Spacing, corners and buttons. | Three pieces that feel right. |

**13. Test it on a real job (5 minutes)**
**Prompt 2: make something**
> Using our design system in [link], make a welcome email for new subscribers. Goal: [what you want it to
> achieve]. Keep it under 120 words.

Then check: does it look like you, does it sound like you, would you send it?

**14. What you get**
**Plain language in. On-brand draft out.**
- **Email:** ask for a welcome email. Get the layout, copy and image slots in your brand.
- **Ad:** ask for three hooks. Get three ads in the right size, in your fonts and colours.
- **Deck:** ask for a retailer pitch. Get slides that look like they came from your design team.
A few edits from the finish line, not a blank page.

**15. What changes for the team**
**Why this is worth 30 minutes**
- Marketers move faster and try more ideas.
- Designers stop policing small mistakes and spend their time on the big work.
- The brand looks the same in every channel, even at pace.
- New joiners, freelancers and agencies are on brand from day one.

**16. Roles**
**Designers set the standard. The system scales it.**
| Designers decide | The system handles |
|---|---|
| The brand, the standards and the big ideas. | Applying the rules to every email, ad and deck. |
| What good looks like. | Keeping it consistent at pace. |
| Which exceptions are allowed. | Getting you to a good first draft. |

**17. Be honest**
**What to watch out for**
- It gets you a few edits from the finish line. Review everything.
- Check the system with your designer before the whole team uses it.
- Figma plans limit how much the connection can do. [Check your plan]
- Keep the brand book as the source of truth, and update it first.
- Keep customer data out of it unless you know where it goes.

**Build it for one asset you make every month.**

# Creative strategy, for founders

Reworked script for the guide. It replaces two decks: "Message, hook, angle, format, offer" and
"Meta GEM and Andromeda".

- **Who it is for:** founders of consumer brands who spend on paid social, or are about to, and keep hearing
  that creative strategy is the thing to get right.
- **The promise:** understand what creative strategy is, why it has become the priority, and how to brief it
  well, without becoming a creative director.
- **What changed from the old decks:** this is now a primer on the basics for founders. The five boxes are
  the "how". GEM and Andromeda are no longer a guide of their own. They are the "why this matters now"
  section, slides 5 to 8, because they are algorithm updates and will be superseded.
- Text in [square brackets] is for you to check or fill in before it goes out.

**2. Before you start**
**What this guide covers**
- What creative strategy is, in plain terms.

- How to test ideas and read the results as a founder.
Reading time: about 15 minutes. Useful with or without an agency.

**3. The short version**
**Creative strategy is deciding what to say, to whom, and why they should care, before anyone makes anything.**
It is not the edit, the font or the music. It is the thinking that makes those choices easy.

**4. Why founders get this wrong**
**Most ad accounts have a production problem that is really a thinking problem.**
- "We need more creative" usually means "we have made the same ad twelve times".
- More versions of one idea do not find new customers.
- You know your customer better than any agency. That makes you the best source of strategy, even if you
  never open an editing tool.

**5. Why it matters now**
**The ad is now the targeting.**
Meta's systems read your creative to decide who sees it. Different ideas reach different people. Small tweaks
to one idea mostly do not. That is why creative strategy has moved from a nice-to-have to the priority.

**6. What changed inside Meta, in plain English**
**Two updates, one consequence**
- **Andromeda** is Meta's ad retrieval system. For each person it narrows tens of millions of possible ads to
  a few thousand, using the ad itself to judge who might care: the image, the video, the words. (Meta
  Engineering, December 2024.)
- **GEM** is Meta's ads foundation model. It does not pick ads. It teaches the models that do, and it learns
  from every ad you run. (Meta Engineering, November 2025.)
- The consequence for you: audience settings do less of the work, and the range of ideas in your ads does more.
These are algorithm updates. The names will change. The principle is likely to stay.

**7. Be careful with sources**
**What Meta has said, and what is opinion**
| Meta's own words | Industry opinion |
|---|---|
| Retrieval narrows tens of millions of ads to a few thousand. | You need 8 to 15 distinct ads per campaign. Sources disagree on the number. |
| GEM is a foundation model that improves the models downstream. | Similar ads get suppressed above a set similarity score. Nobody outside Meta has confirmed one. |
| Conversion gains of 5% on Instagram and 3% on Facebook Feed in Q2 2025. Meta's own figures, with no published method. | Audience targeting now matters much less. |

**8. What counts as a different ad**
| A different ad | The same ad again |
|---|---|
| A different problem it solves | A new headline on the same image |
| A different type of customer | A new background colour |
| A different format: video, static or creator | The same video with a new caption |
| A different first line or first second | The same layout in a new size |
| A different kind of proof | |

**9. The five decisions**
**Every ad answers five questions.**
Message. Hook. Angle. Format. Offer. If you cannot answer all five in a line each, the ad is not ready to make.

**10. Message**
**The one thing you want them to remember.**
- One sentence. If it needs an "and", it is two messages.
- True, specific and about them, not about you.
- Weak: "Our tea is high quality." Stronger: "One tea that still tastes good on the fifth steep."

**11. Hook**
**The first line, or the first second of a video.**
- It only has to earn the next three seconds.
- Five kinds: a question, a number, a problem, a surprise, or someone like them.
- Weak: "Introducing our new tea." Stronger: "You are throwing away tea you could drink four more times."

**12. Angle**
**The reason to care, right now. Your message seen from one person's side.**
- One message can have many angles. This is where "different ads" come from.
- Common angles: a problem, a wish, proof, a comparison, a story, an objection.
- Message: tea lasts five steeps. Angles: save money, less waste, slow evenings, the taste test.

**13. Format**
**How the idea is delivered: static, video, carousel, creator, email.**
- Choose the format after the angle, not before.
- A comparison suits a carousel. A story suits video. A claim suits a static with proof.

**14. Offer**
**What they get, and what they do next.**
- A discount is one offer, not the only one: a bundle, a trial, free delivery, a guarantee.
- Make the next step obvious: one button, one action.
- Weak: "Shop now." Stronger: "Try the five-steep tea. Free delivery, and a refund if it does not last."

**15. The order to decide in**
1. Message first.
2. Angle second: choose the person and the reason.
3. Hook third: write the first line for that angle.
4. Format fourth: choose what shows it best.
5. Offer last: decide what happens next.
Message and angle are strategy. Hook and format are craft. The offer is the ask. As a founder, own the first
two and review the rest.

**16. Worked example**
**One brief, three ads**
Brief: one tea that lasts five steeps, for people who waste money on tea. Free delivery, and a refund if it does not last.
- **Save money.** Hook: "You are throwing away tea." Format: static with a cost-per-cup comparison.
- **Slow evenings.** Hook: "The fifth cup is the best one." Format: 15-second video.
- **Proof.** Hook: "We tested it against four big brands." Format: carousel.

**17. The one-page brief**
- Product and customer, one line each.
- Message: one sentence.
- Three angles.
- Two hook options for each angle.
- A format for each ad.
- Offer and the next step.
- What must be true: claims, legal, tone.
If it needs two pages, the message is not clear yet. Hand this to your team, a freelancer or an agency.

**18. Build a library, not a batch**
- Pick four to six distinct ideas your customers would recognise.
- Make two or three versions of each: a different hook, the same idea.
- Mix formats: static, video, carousel, creator content.
- Name every ad by idea, format and hook, so you can read results by idea.
- Add two new ideas every fortnight and retire the tired ones.

**19. Testing and reading results, as a founder**
- Test in this order: angle, then format, then hook, then offer. Change one at a time.
- Judge at account level first: cost per purchase and return across the whole account.
- Then compare by idea, not by single ad.
- Give each idea a fair run. [Set your own minimum spend and number of days.]
- Write down what you learned. The library is the asset.

**20. Habits to drop**
- Making five versions of one idea and calling it a test.
- Splitting budget across lots of tiny audiences.
- Changing ads and budgets every day.
- Judging an ad after 24 hours.
- Killing a whole idea after one weak version.
- Choosing a format because it is easy, not because it fits.

**22. The founder's checklist**
- I can say our message in one sentence.
- We have at least [4 to 6] distinct ideas live, not versions.
- Every ad has a one-page brief behind it.
- Ads are named by idea, format and hook.
- New ideas arrive every fortnight.
- We have a written list of what we have learned.

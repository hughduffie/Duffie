# Growth culture is as important as any growth tactic

Outline for a new guide. First draft, written from the title for Hugh's comments.

- **Who it is for:** founders and marketing leads at consumer brands who have tried the tactics (new channels,
  new ads, a new agency) and found the results do not stick.
- **The promise:** understand what a growth culture is, check whether you have one, and set up the habits
  that build it in a small team, in the next month.
- **The argument:** tactics are easy to copy and they wear out. The habits that find the next tactic are
  harder to copy and they compound. A team that tests, learns and shares every week will beat a team with a
  better playbook and no rhythm.

**4. Why tactics wear out**
**Every tactic you can read about, your competitors can read about too.**
- Channels get more expensive as more brands use them.
- Ad formats and platform rules change every few months.
- A tactic that worked for another brand was built on their customer, their product and their timing.
What lasts is the ability to find the next thing that works, faster than the last one stops.

**5. The short version**
**A growth culture is a team that tests, learns and shares, every week, without being asked.**
It is not a growth team, a job title or a tool. It is how decisions get made.

**6. What it looks like**
**Five habits**
- **One goal everyone can name.** The team knows the number that matters this quarter, and why.
- **A steady flow of tests.** Small, cheap experiments run every week, not one big launch a quarter.
- **Learning is written down.** Every test ends with a line on what it showed, win or lose.
- **Ideas come from anywhere.** Customer service, the warehouse and finance see things marketing does not.
- **Decisions are quick and close to the work.** People do not wait a fortnight for sign-off on a small test.

**7. Signs you do not have one yet**
| What you hear | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "We tried that, it did not work." | Nobody knows why, so it cannot be fixed. |
| "Let's wait until the founder is back." | Decisions sit with one person. |
| "Marketing owns growth." | Product, service and ops are not involved. |
| "We need a big idea." | Small tests are not valued. |
| "That test failed, so we stopped." | Losing tests are treated as mistakes, not information. |

**8. Tactics and culture need each other**
**A good tactic in a weak culture is a one-off. A good culture finds the next tactic.**
| Tactic | Culture |
|---|---|
| What you do this month. | How you decide what to do next month. |
| Can be copied from a guide. | Has to be built inside the team. |
| Wears out. | Compounds. |
This is not an argument against tactics. The other guides on this site are full of them. It is an argument for
the habits that make them work.

**9. The goal**
**Pick one number for the quarter.**
- Choose the number that best shows customers getting value: repeat orders, subscriptions kept, first orders.
- Say why it matters, in one sentence.
- Put it where everyone sees it, every week.
- Keep it for the whole quarter, even when it is uncomfortable.
Revenue matters, but it is the result. Pick the number that drives it.

**10. The backlog**
**One list of ideas, open to everyone**
- One shared sheet or board. Anyone can add an idea.
- Each idea gets one line: what we think will happen, and why.
- Score ideas the same way each week, for example by likely impact, confidence and effort.
- Pick the next tests from the top. Say no to the rest for now, not forever.

**11. The weekly growth meeting**
**Thirty minutes, same time every week**
1. The number: where it is and what moved it. (5 minutes)
2. Last week's tests: what we learned, one line each. (10 minutes)
3. This week's tests: what we will run, who owns each one. (10 minutes)
4. Anything blocked. (5 minutes)
No slides. Same agenda every week. Start on time even if people are missing.

**12. How to run a test**
**Small, quick and written down before it starts**
- What we will change.
- What we expect to happen, and by how much.
- How we will know: the number and the date we check it.
- What we will do if it works, and if it does not.
If you cannot fill in all four lines, it is not ready to run.

**13. Losing tests**
**A test that loses is not a mistake. A test with no write-up is.**
- Most tests will not win. That is normal. [Check: find a source you are happy to quote on typical win rates, or leave this as opinion.]
- Thank people for a clean loss as much as a win.
- Ask "what did we learn", never "whose fault was it".
- Keep a learning log. After six months it is one of the most useful documents in the business.

**14. Share it widely**
**Learning only compounds if people see it.**
- Send a short weekly note to the whole company: the number, the tests, what we learned.
- Share the losses as well as the wins.
- Name the people who suggested the ideas, whatever team they are in.
- Invite the next idea.

**15. Ideas from outside marketing**
**The people closest to customers see things first.**
| Team | What they see |
|---|---|
| Customer service | The questions and complaints that stop people buying again. |
| Warehouse and ops | What gets returned, damaged or delayed. |
| Finance | Which products, channels and offers make money after costs. |
| Sales and retail | What buyers and shop staff say about you. |
Ask each team for one idea a month. Run at least one of them.

**16. The founder's part**
**Set the goal, protect the rhythm, then step back.**
- Choose the goal and explain why.
- Turn up to the weekly meeting, and do not run it.
- Ask what was learned before asking what was sold.
- Let the team run small tests without asking you.
- Keep the big, hard-to-reverse decisions. Hand over the small, reversible ones.

**17. For marketing leads**
**Build it from the middle**
- You do not need a founder's permission to run a weekly meeting with your own team.
- Start with your own tests and write-ups. Others copy what visibly works.
- Bring one idea a month from another team, and credit them.
- Share the weekly note upwards as well as across.

**18. With agencies and freelancers**
**Bring them into the rhythm, not around it.**
- Share the quarter's number with them.
- Ask them to bring tests to the weekly meeting, with a hypothesis.
- Ask for learnings, not only reports.
More on this in the guide: Why I like in-person meetings, and how to get the best out of agencies.

**19. With Claude**
**Use it to keep the rhythm going, not to replace the thinking.**
> Here is our learning log for the last quarter: [paste]. Group the tests by theme. What patterns do you see?
> What have we not tested that the log suggests we should? List five ideas, one line each, with the reason.

> Here is this week's growth meeting notes: [paste]. Write a short note for the whole company: the number,
> the tests, what we learned, and one ask. Under 150 words. Plain language.

Review it before it goes out. Keep anything about customers or money inside the business.

**20. What to expect**
**Be honest about the timing**
- The first month feels slow. People are learning the format.
- Most early tests will be small and some will lose.
- The value shows up after a few months, when the learning log starts to answer questions before they are asked.
- It will not fix a product customers do not want. It will tell you sooner.

**21. The team checklist**
- We can all name this quarter's number.
- We have one shared backlog of ideas, and anyone can add to it.
- We meet for thirty minutes every week, with the same agenda.
- Every test has a written hypothesis before it starts.
- Every test has a one-line write-up when it ends.
- We share what we learned with the whole company each week.
- At least one idea a month comes from outside marketing.

**22. Start small**
**Run the weekly meeting for four weeks before you change anything else.**
